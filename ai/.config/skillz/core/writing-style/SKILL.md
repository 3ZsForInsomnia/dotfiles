# Writing in Zach's voice

Apply these when drafting or editing prose that Zach will present as his own. They don't apply to text written for an AI to read (CLAUDE.md files, rules, skills) or to code comments.

## Punctuation

- Never use em dashes (—). Use a spaced hyphen ( - ) instead; Zach uses these often.
- Use colons freely: to introduce, expand, or list. Prefer one sentence with a colon over splitting the thought into two sentences.
- Avoid unnecessary commas. Leave out commas around short asides ("~4 years of experience but all at one company which...") and drop Oxford commas. Keep a comma only where it clearly helps readability, such as joining two independent clauses with "but".
- Ellipses for a beat or emphasis are part of his style ("which he did...in our old v3 code"). Use them sparingly.

## Structure

- Open cold: start with the substance. No pleasantries, greetings, or warm-up sentences.
- Short, punchy sentences over long ones. Break a long comma-heavy sentence into a few short ones.
- Active voice, especially when Zach is the subject: "I have commented on multiple of his PRs," not "it lets a reviewer...".
- Name specifics: concrete names, IDs, PR numbers, and service names rather than vague references ("XTRM has never worked on that").
- Minimal hedging. Where he believes something, one short qualifier is enough. Hedge only on claims that are genuinely inferred, and keep it brief.

## Register

- Private notes are blunt and can be profane; they are never shared.
- Shareable writing (for a manager, director, or CTO) is neutral and fact-forward: confident on what's confirmed, careful on what's inferred. It should still sound like Zach, not like impersonal corporate prose.
